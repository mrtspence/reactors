A minion is an individual -- what you have sketched (yardhand, fireman) is a job for a minion to peform in an operation.

You don't unlock a fireman -- that is just the job that your minion performs in your operation.

You unlock Jim (a human who starts with xzy stats and has abc tags) or Elowynne (an elf with their own stats and tags). Each of those minions are basically their own template, that can be upgraded with other blueprints (things like certification courses that can give them stat bumps or new tags).

Also equipment will have to be another abstraction. It has its own sets of blueprints that need to be unlocked but it is anything that help a minion perform their jobs -- things like specialized tools or protective gear -- that is not part of the minion themselves. Equipment will provide stat bonuses and add tags (will let us later add things like exposed-flame lamps that could cause gas explosions in mines but also just something like having the shovel tag should give you a huge bonus in moving coal around or scooping up ore -- things that will be checked in the simulation at those relevant positions).

To keep things simple but flexible, minion equipment should each belong to one of 3 slots, with only one equipment allowed per slot for a given minion:
* Tool Set -- the things a minion carries with them. A tool set called Crude Miner's Tools might be nothing more than a primitive stone pick and a candle. This could grant the minion the tags: { mining_effectiveness: 0.25, darkvision: 0.1, open_flame: true }. In many mine operations, there will be nodes for workers that will make a minion-based check to see how much ore they knock loose that would only produce output if the minion there had the mining_effectiveness tag with a value above 0 and some light was present (even an ogre isnt getting ore out of a seam without a tool and nobody is doing any work if they can't see -- but some races like a dwarf might be able to see in the dark and can come with darkvision: 0.5 on the minion themselves), their output multiplied by their mining_effectiveness and darkvision (for a punishing x 0.25 x 0.1). Better mines might provide lighting while they have power, etc etc.
* Gear -- what they are wearing. Could be things that might allow a minion to withstand hazards like high heat, toxic fumes, etc or a powered exoskeleton that gives them huge stat boosts. Same as before -- stat modifiers and tags describe all we need here.
* Utility -- a special slot for more niche or specialized equipment. It might be a lucky amulet that gives a tag or a tiny stat boost or it might be a way to get a rebreather onto a minion who you want to otherwise not have to wear a hazmat suit or something similar.

Before starting a match, we will need another screen that allows you to assign your minions to the match, swap their equipment in and out (equipment needs to be unlocked per-minion, so Jim and Elowynne each have their own yardhand equipment blueprints), position them in the starting roles, and then commence the match.

At some point soon, we will need to model minion position and minion transit -- and we will need to honestly confront when that is best handled. It might be sooner than later as minion injuries are also related to where they are relative to a failing machine or a hazard in the environment. My suspicion is that it is the next thing to tackle after this modularization pass.

For something like a mine, moving minions through the mine itself is actually one of the largest logistical hurdles and failing to model it would be a gross oversimplification (and would undercut the need for devices like lifts and man-engines and make things like repairs or shuffling around tired minions trivial). Realistically, we want the simplest possible model that will still work, but since we already have conductance and nodes that describe volumes of air and other materials, it might be as simple as roughing in the geometry in large volumes by adding a local environment node to describe what is in that volume of space. The local environment volumes could be as large or as small as we want and we just need to keep track of them (this way we could have things like a 'main shaft' which has its own custom volumes and connects to drifts and crosscuts on each level -- and we could use an equivalent to our tagged transports for materials but for minions that could dictate who can go where and how fast -- eg a gargoyle might be able to just fly up to a raised platform in a refinery but someone without the flying tag would have to walk). We would then need to track which volume a given minion is in and which operation parts belong to which (does not seem difficult) and an abstract map of how they connect.

This minion movement part and refinement of our spacial conception is a serious undertaking, so let's get back to it after the easier stuff has been handled.
