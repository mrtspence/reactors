# `spec/`

```sh
bundle exec rspec                                        # everything (~2.5 min)
bundle exec rspec spec/reactor_sim/conservation_spec.rb  # one file
```

## Two helpers, and the split is load-bearing

- **`spec_helper`** — deliberately Rails-free. It must never load Rails, boot the app, or touch
  a database. Simulation specs use this. A `spec_helper` that quietly booted Rails would let
  the `lib/reactor_sim` boundary rot without anyone noticing.
- **`rails_helper`** — for specs that genuinely exercise the web tier: request specs,
  ViewComponent specs, channel specs. Requires `spec_helper` first.

`.rspec` requires `spec_helper` only, so a sim spec needs no database and no services.

## The specs that guard the invariants

Each of the four invariants has a spec that fails loudly when it is broken. **If you are
changing the engine, run these before anything else** — a violation does not crash, it produces
a match that quietly cannot be recovered or replayed.

The table below is a snapshot; `ls spec/reactor_sim/` is the truth. **A new spec file gets a
row here in the same commit** — this table is how a reader finds out a guard already exists
instead of writing a second one.

| Spec | Guards |
|---|---|
| `purity_spec` | Boots the sim in a bare Ruby subprocess **and** sweeps the source statically |
| `determinism_spec` | Same seed + commands → byte-identical state; snapshot round-trip; idempotence |
| `graph_spec` | Shuffles node and link order and compares digests |
| `transport_spec` | That a conduit holds nothing, delivers its **full** rating, and leaves no node alternating — plus path resolution, chains, cycles and dead ends. Also the gas solver: two vessels equalise at **every** conductance, flow reverses when the gradient does, a check valve refuses to, and the same interval settles the same at any `dt` |
| `conservation_spec` | Mass and energy balance against the ledger |
| `dust_spec` | **What turns an accident into a catastrophe.** The load-bearing examples are the ones about *settled* dust: it is made by **cutting** (zero with nobody at the face), the ventilation does not touch it, and once it has settled a district has no way to get rid of it. That middle claim is asserted as dust **per kilogram cut**, not as a total, and the reason is worth knowing: once bad air drained fatigue, an unventilated district made 1.9% less dust — because its hewer cannot breathe, so he cuts less. The ratio is identical to six decimals, which is the claim; the totals never will be again — dust is tagged `dust`, which is neither `gas` nor `solid`, so nothing in the mine will carry it while it still burns perfectly well. Then Senghenydd: with the fan running and the gas cleared, the firedamp alone could not do it and **the dust consumes every kilogram of itself** doing it instead. Stone dusting leaves most of it unburned, spends stores that run down, and deliberately **does not stop the gas burning** — it is why ignitions stopped becoming disasters, not why they stopped happening |
| `roof_spec` | **The post whose entire output is nothing going wrong.** Hewing exposes ground, timbering supports it, and the roadway wears on the **difference** — so cutting at 40 with nobody timbering is exactly as dangerous as cutting at 100 with the face 60% supported, which one example asserts directly because a *ratio* would make an idle face consume timber to stand still. Gives the gradient the operations guide asks every machine for — read off `integrity` after 200 ticks (**1.000 / 0.911 / 0.821** across full, half and no timbering) rather than by waiting 6,000 for a fall, because the gradient *is* the erosion rate and the fall is where it ends up. The fall itself comes from a road **seeded part-worn**, which is what a district driven all shift is really in: unsupported it comes in and kills the putter, and the same worn road with somebody timbering it stays up. Severity scales with how far the face had run ahead (rank 13 against 7), so the same fall is worse on a face that was being driven hard. The difference-not-ratio example is equal to eight significant figures rather than bit-identical — the two arrive at the same exposure by different lever arithmetic |
| `cage_spec` | **Buying your way out of the walk**, which is the purchase the whole spatial model exists to make worth making. A whim brings no cage and no lever; a cage winder brings both. Uncalled, the shift still takes the ladders — fitting one takes nothing away, it only adds a faster way that has to be asked for — and called, they get down in under half the time. What it costs turned out to be **air, not coal**: the cage is on the same line shaft as the fan, so calling it slows the shaft and the district gets gassier while the shift is being wound — asserted at the upcast, where the mechanism is (2.619 kg/tick to 1.686 within 50 ticks), with the gas on the gauge as its consequence. And with the supply cut it falls back to the ladders rather than stranding anybody: awful, and survivable. **Its journeys are real and timed, not constructed, because the time is the claim** — and they are cheap because the post timed is the *putter's*, at the near end of the road: 600 ticks by ladder, 281 by man engine, 167 by cage, the whole tech tree inside one short window. The face posts are 1,267 and 834 and would say nothing more |
| `firedamp_spec` | **The thing the ventilation is for.** Same mine, same gas, one lever: the fan holds the district at a trace, and stopping it lets the gas build past the explosive limit. Then the decision the whole period turns on — safety lamps and it never lights however gassy it gets; naked lights and it does. **Its own crew rather than `ReferenceCrew`**, because these examples are about *who was standing where when it went up* and a day-labourer takes fourteen simulated minutes to reach the face: the explosion happens without them, which is correct behaviour and a useless assertion. Also that the blower vents **harder into a clear district than a gassy one**, which is what makes it pressure-driven rather than a fixed rate, and that the flame cap stays **prose** — a number there would be a different game |
| `mine_spec` | The second operation, end to end. Driven from a steady supply rather than a real engine — the coupling is proved in `coupling_spec`, and a mine spec that first has to raise steam measures the wrong machine. The claims the spatial work exists for: **nobody is at the face on the tick they are sent**, no coal is won until somebody actually arrives, and the walk lands them at their posts on schedule. Then the signature failure — **cut the supply and the workings drown**, because drainage is on the same shaft as everything else. Its conservation examples found both of this stage's real bugs: air reversing into an unbooked port, and a pump lifting water for free with the shaft stopped. **Every example pins `ground:`** — how fiery and how wet a pit is is drawn per match, so an unpinned mine spec measures the draw rather than the machine; `describe "the ground"` is the one block that exercises the drawing, and asserts that two matches differ, that each seep varies independently of the others, and that a seed reproduces its own pit exactly |
| `lever_travel_spec` | **Phase 0, which was inert on every machine in the game until the mine's valves shipped a finite `stiffness`.** `actuate` returned on its second line for every lever, so `crew_multiplier` was computed and discarded and the whole phase passed its specs by never running. Asserts the valve travels rather than snapping, arrives at its rated speed, and takes half as long to go half as far; that an **effort station still snaps**, which is a decision rather than an oversight; that a strong hand works a shutter faster than a weak one and faster than nobody; and that **an unattended lever travels at its rated speed**, which is the decided rule rather than a default. Then the invariant most at risk, since a lever now has a position no command sets: re-sending the same target mid-travel leaves the digest identical, because a command writes `target` and never `actual` |
| `passage_spec` | **Where somebody is, and how long it takes them to be somewhere else.** The load-bearing example is the *negative* one: an operation declaring no `passages:` assigns instantly, exactly as before, because geometry is opt-in and the steam engine must not have acquired a walk to the firehole. Then arrival timed against real distance, routing through an intermediate place that carries no lever at all, a gated passage refusing somebody who cannot fit and admitting somebody who can, and distance **carrying over between passages within one tick** so a high `time_scale` is not silently capped at one passage per tick. `assign_minion` stays absolute: replayed mid-journey it changes nothing, and a change of orders does not send a walker back to the start. **Its rig cannot use `ReferenceCrew`** — see the `pace` trap below |
| `coupling_spec` | **Work crossing between two operations**, which nothing did before it. That each side's books close *independently* as well as together — the source pays through `joules_to_work`, the sink takes in through `joules_imported` — and that the transfer cannot depend on the order operations are visited in, since `Match#exchange!` reads every source before writing any sink. The brownout is the shape worth keeping: cut the supply and the shaft **holds its equilibrium for ~880 ticks** on the buffer already in hand, then coasts down on windage, which is the behaviour a ledger figure could not produce. Also that a coupling between operations on different `time_scale`s is **refused** — a joule is a joule but a rate is not, and the fast side would be starved in proportion. **Its tolerance is relative to energy handled, not to the opening balance**: a sink starts empty, so normalising against its opening figure turns a relative tolerance into an absolute one and fails on ordinary float noise |
| `thermal_spec` | Relaxation, ambient loss, rebalancing. Also **radiation**, where the load-bearing example is an *identity*: `h_rad × (T − T_amb)` must equal `εσA(T⁴ − T_amb⁴)` to float precision at every temperature, because if the factoring drifts then every radiative figure in the engine is quietly wrong. Plus the two that justify the approach over an explicit `T⁴` — nothing overshoots its sink at `dt = 10⁶`, and a node declaring no emissivity is **bit-identical** to one from before radiation existed |
| `transport_affinity_spec` | `Node#transport_affinity` — that a neutral opinion is **bit-identical** to no opinion, that a weight changes the mix and never the total on a rate-driven path, the max-within-a-port / product-across-ports combining rule, and that `Boiler` declares a steam quality rather than the unguessable multiplier. **Built on an inert two-liquid registry**: a rig made of real water measures evaporation as well as apportionment, and drifted 90% → 82% while nothing under test had moved |
| `steam_engine_spec` → "priming and hydraulic lock" | **One `skip`, and the marker is deliberate.** The demonstration was withdrawn, not broken: with an honest swell signal the same run peaks at occupancy 0.163 and the cylinder survives, so what is unproven is that a **reachable operating point** exists where the drum is wet enough and the crank still turning. `skip` rather than `pending` because `pending` executes the body, and this was 6,200 ticks of a run expected to demonstrate nothing. The mechanism itself is proven in `obstruction_spec`; the TODO names `EngineRig` as the tool to settle it, since a constructed state can be put at a wet drum *and* a turning crank at once — which is precisely what the long run could not reach |
| `entrainment_spec` | `Arbiter.entrained` — the **pressure-driven** half of settlement. That the gas figure the solve settled survives any weight the clamp allows, that liquid is additive but bounded by the bore, and that a path with no declared opinion still passes what is in it. **Its rig declares a `conductance:` and that is the entire point** — `transport_affinity_spec`'s does not, which is why all 13 of its examples test the other branch |
| `obstruction_spec` | `Concerns::Obstructs` — occupancy against a **characteristic** volume, the derived top-dead-centre pressure, hydraulic lock graded by the driveline's stored energy, and that a relief valve sensing the wrong quantity stays shut on a state that would destroy the part. Also the concern's second caller, a bed choked by its own ash — **if it only ever had one it would not have earned a file** |
| `assembly_spec` | Slots, parts, loadouts and the validator. **Two halves on purpose**: a deliberately tiny two-vessel registry, so which rule fired is never in doubt, then the same machinery pointed at the real engine — a mechanism that works on a toy and not on a real machine has proved nothing. Guards the id contract (`provides:`), the flat-namespace collision naming *both* slots, reachability through the real `Path` router, and **the error/warning split**, which is the game's risk/reward axis rather than two severities. Also the loadout's three snapshot traps: string part ids, a partial loadout re-defaulting, and `:none`. **`OPTIONAL` is written out rather than derived from the slots**, so a slot quietly becoming optional — or quietly ceasing to be — fails a spec instead of passing one; the converse example empties every *required* slot and insists each is refused. Also guards **`PANEL_ORDER`**: that a gauge a part *supplied* still lands where the panel says rather than at the end, and that every catalogued gauge has a place. Note that "takes its own pieces with it" counts every list plus the diagnostics, not `fragment.nodes` — an instrument part brings no nodes at all |
| `content_spec` | Eager validation, mass balance, latent-heat encoding. Also that **every `:structural` material carries a `max_temperature_k`** — a missing one is silent and total, and is why over-temperature fatigue existed from the day `Wearing` landed and had never once fired |
| `steam_engine_spec` → "going without the safety devices" | **One example**, and it is the only "going without" claim that is about the *startup* rather than about a stage: with no blower a cold stack has no buoyancy, so the fire never even establishes — 636 K against 1,013 K at 100 ticks. The safety valve, the tube bundle and the cylinder relief all moved to `engine_stages_spec`, where each is a 60-tick claim on a constructed state. Assert the *relationship*, never a pinned figure |
| `engine_stages_spec` → "water in the cylinder" | **Warming through, decomposed** — the composite is the sum of these four and so does not need its own run, and could not have one anyway: a cold cylinder on a *working* engine reads occupancy 0.011 rather than the 0.859 a slow warm-up reaches, because a turning engine sweeps the water out. The four: a cold casting condenses 3× what a warm one does; the cocks drain it **at the price of the steam that goes with it** (139 rpm against 171); revolutions are what empty it, so a cracked regulator at 68 rpm gets **wetter** (0.430 → 0.513) while a working engine sweeps itself dry (→ 0.001); and 10 kg of water puts compression at 3,413 kPa against the relief valve's 912, which it survives with the valve and loses the head without. **The load stays on in all four** — at `load_demand: 0` the wheel runs away and bursts inside 40 ticks, and then every reading is about the wheel |
| `crown_sheet_spec` | The low-water hazard: that the plate is at water temperature while covered and costs nothing, that starving the feed uncovers it and blows the fusible plug, that **the plug goes before the plate does** (620 K against 750), that it stays melted once melted, and that the gauge glass reads high while the plate is already bare. Also the other half of the plug's story: **take it off and the same run explodes the drum** and spills it, which is the end-to-end proof that the failure model fires in a real machine rather than only in a rig. **Requires `reactor_sim` directly** — `spec_helper` alone does not load the sim. **The most expensive file in the suite at ~12 min**, because every example runs the engine into the hazard from cold |
| `diagnostic_spec` | The instrument chain, `record` vs `read`, `distortion?`. Also that the water-gauge **tiers are a real progression** — mean misreading against the spectator's truth, ordered rather than pinned, because pinning figures makes every balance change a failure. It exists because both tiers shipped broken the first time: try-cocks never moved at all, and the reflex glass cut noise below the display's own precision. **~90 s**, four engine runs |
| `player_view_spec` | The wire protocol — that merging deltas equals receiving a full view |
| `minion_spec` | The crew, and the actuation seam nothing else can reach |
| `event_spec` | The durable record's contract, none of which was specced before it existed. That the vocabulary has no duplicate and — **derived by scanning the library for `Event.build(type: :name)`, never written out** — no entry nothing emits, because a type a consumer waits on forever is indistinguishable from an achievement nobody has earned. That imposes one rule on emitters: **spell the type literally**, so a computed `type:` cannot slip past the check. And the load-bearing one: **replaying from a snapshot re-emits the same events in the same order**, which is what makes `(match_id, run_id, operation_id, tick, seq)` a safe dedupe key and at-least-once delivery harmless |
| `services/progression_digest_spec` | The fold from log to permanent record, driven by hand-built records rather than a broker — what is worth guarding is the fold, and none of it is about Kafka. Point facts award once and are idempotent under redelivery; an interval can be spoiled, can re-open without restarting a clean one, and **cannot close against a different `run_id`**; meter readings are absolute, so an older reading arriving late never walks a total backwards. **The helper's `stringify_keys` is load-bearing**: `node: "flywheel"` under a Symbol key silently matches nothing |
| `services/event_pipeline_spec` | The whole chain on a real cold start: engine → the envelope the producer puts round each fact → `JSON` → the digest → an award. **The only spec that can catch a mismatch between hops**, which is how this system actually breaks — neither side wrong on its own, nothing raised, and an achievement that silently never fires. It earned its keep immediately: `raised_steam_from_cold_alone` was awarded on every ordinary start, because the igniter fires at tick 1 and the fire catches at tick 2, so the only heater event fell outside its own window. Also holds the volume budget — **under 20 events in 1700 ticks** |
| `failure_spec` | The failure contract independent of any machine: that `failure` is a **mode**, that `broken?` derives from it (and answers for a node with no `Wearing`), that a mode survives JSON **as a Symbol**, that escalation only moves forward and emits only on a transition, and that the boiler's mode splits on conditions rather than on `cause`. **Escalation is proved on a real part, not just on `escalate_to`** — a cylinder worn to `scored_bore` that then hydro-locks becomes `blown_head`, which is the only thing that shows the mechanism is reachable at all. The identity assertion is the point: with the normalisation disabled, the round-trip *digest* comparison in the same file still passes. **Walks every catalogued operation on every chassis** (44 wearing nodes today) and fails any part left on `Wearing::GENERIC_FAILURE`, which is the guard that keeps the fallback from becoming a silent off switch. Also the `Breach` rig: shut costs nothing, opens by the mode's size, stays shut for a mode it does not name, books `mass_spilled` rather than `mass_vented`, and conserves both balances across a burst. **Two things about it are easy to assert wrongly** — a drum empties to *ambient*, not to zero, and total spill *saturates*, so breach sizes must be compared by what is left rather than by what came out |
| `bearing_spec` | The hot box on a rig: that an oiled journal never wears, that a dry one **wipes before it seizes rather than instead of** (a threshold sliding with durability lets it seize at 481.7 K on a 0.63 integrity and the warning rung never happens), that both thresholds come from the material rather than a constant, and that a seizure **stops the shaft** — which neither `Tick#stress` nor `settle_drive` does for you, so a regression fails silently and in the safe direction. Two things about the rig are load-bearing: starvation is a bearing built `oil_charge_kg: 0.0` rather than state surgery, and its `static_load_n` is deliberately heavy, because at the default load a dry journal plateaus at 461 K — above the service limit, below the melting point — and wipes and then sits there. The wipe-derate example runs on an **oiled** bearing for the same reason: a dry one has no film left to lose. Also the oil round: that a bearing takes **only what it is short of** however much is on offer — the regression that silently disabled the whole mechanic, because `Intent.none` when full means the *path* drives the flow and a 1.2 kg journal fills to its housing's 8.9 kg — that oil is spent by sliding distance rather than by the clock, and that `mass_consumed` balances both books |
| `ignition_spec` | That a fire needs a spark, spreads in a cold box, and dies without air. Also **the flammability range**: a gaseous fuel carries a flame only between `lean_fraction` and `rich_fraction` of the volume and does nothing however hot the room is either side, burning a mixture back down through its lean limit puts the fire out, and a reaction declaring no range is untouched — a fuel bed is not a mixture, and a solid at 800 kg/m³ occupies almost none of the volume it sits in |
| `performance_spec` | ~55 ms/tick at 100 nodes against a 250 ms budget |
| `steam_engine_spec` | **Only what no constructed state can stand in for**, now that every per-stage claim lives in `engine_stages_spec`. **Nothing here runs past 100 ticks** except a 50-tick snapshot, and the rule that got it there is that *a whole startup is the composition of the stage claims* — running one re-proves each slice in sequence at a hundred times the price. What survives: that a 292 K firebox can be brought to a fire that **sustains itself** (alight at tick 11, igniter out at 30, 1,013 K at 100), that an engine with no blower cannot even establish one, conservation through **lighting up** (the one trajectory the five seeded states cannot visit, since all start hot), the architectural thesis and snapshots. The cold-start example is the load-bearing one and **must never be built on `Settled`** — a fixture restored from a hot engine cannot fail when the path to a hot engine breaks. If a claim here seems to need thousands of ticks, it is a composition of stage claims and belongs there |

## The delivery tier

`spec/runner/` and `spec/components/` use `rails_helper`.

| Spec | Guards |
|---|---|
| `runner/match_runner_spec` | The tick barrier and command routing — sim vs runner-addressed, and that a bad record cannot kill the loop. Also the two halves of a reset: that a command **following** one in the same batch lands on the rebuilt match rather than the discarded object (the ordering the shared log exists to give), and that the new run **names the one it replaced**, so a watching client adopts it instead of taking it for a second runner |
| `requests/commands_spec` | Ingress validation and status codes; that the operation id comes from the **route** rather than the body, and that addressing a different machine sends the command there |
| `requests/operator_identity_spec` | **The ownership rule, end to end, and mostly the half that refuses.** A check whose only live call site is the one saying yes is a check that rots — the `DevPlayer.earn` versus `grant` argument one layer out. Asserts that a non-owner is turned away from the console, the levers, the outfitting and crew screens, and a reset; that **"not yours" and "no such thing" are byte-identical from outside**, which is the whole point of answering 404 rather than 403 for something you cannot see; and that with the hatch open one developer drives **two operations in one match** while `owner_id` stays untouched |
| `models/operation_spec` | Who owns a machine. That provisioning is idempotent and **never reassigns an existing one** — fitting parts, posting a crew and resetting all rebuild a machine and none may transfer it, which is why ownership is its own row rather than a column on `loadouts` — that a machine with no owner is refused (a gate with no subject fails open), and that the bypass widens `operable_by?` without writing anything |
| `simulation/operator_spec` | **The boot that refuses to open the hatch.** Loads the real initializer against a stubbed environment rather than reimplementing its rule, because a spec that restated it would pass while the initializer was wrong. `REACTOR_OPERATOR_BYPASS` set outside development **raises**, so a misconfigured production fails to start instead of serving every operation to everybody. One trap: `Rails.env.local?` needs `ActiveSupport::EnvironmentInquirer` — a plain `StringInquirer` answers it through `method_missing` as *"is this string 'local'"*, false for development, so the stub refused a boot the real Rails allows |
| `requests/loadouts_spec` | The outfitting screen, and **the order behind the button — validate, store, reset**. Guards the two things that order prevents: a build the validator refused reaching the database, and a refused build reaching the runner. Also that the loadout rides **inside** the reset command rather than being referenced by it, that an unchecked slot arrives as an explicit nil rather than re-defaulting, and that the panel follows the loadout (19 instruments → 17 with the safety valve off). Plus **the second validator**: a part the player has not unlocked is refused *when posted directly*, because the dropdown filter is a courtesy and the form is a plain POST — and a locked part that is already fitted is still shown, flagged, since hiding it would report an error about something the player cannot see. **`Assembly`'s own verdict stays `ok?` throughout**, which is the assertion that keeps ownership out of the simulation. Finally the shapes `permit!` would admit — an Array, a Hash, a bare String, a number — each of which reaches `Array#to_sym` and 500s on the draft action |
| `runner/view_broadcaster_spec` | The wire envelope, `prev_tick` chaining, and the resync contract. Plus `run_id` on every view and `supersedes` on a rebuilt one — what lets a client tell a reset from a second runner broadcasting onto the same stream, which tick alone cannot |
| `channels/operation_channel_spec` | Subscription, rejection, and **the backfill** a joining client gets from the durable log — the fix for a spectator one tick behind the flywheel being told nothing had gone wrong. It caught a bug the rescue around it would otherwise have hidden forever: `transmit(kind: …)` passes Ruby **keywords**, not a positional Hash, so it raised and the backfill silently did nothing. Braces are load-bearing. Also that a failure there costs the backfill and not the subscription, and that only the newest run is carried |
| `models/incident_spec` | That `type` is data rather than Rails' STI column, and that `(run_id, operation_id, tick, seq)` is a **unique index doing dedupe work** — a replayed tick re-emits identically, so redelivery must be an upsert no-op. Plus that backfill takes the *newest* rows before reversing (keeping the oldest fifty would show a joining spectator the start of a match and nothing since) and applies the same severity filter the live feed does |
| `models/match_run_spec` | One row per *build*, not per match, and the retention split: a sweep takes the run's working material — incidents, open attempts, per-run progress — and **leaves awards and lifetime totals alone**, because those are keyed to an owner rather than to a run. Both halves asserted, since the split is the policy |
| `components/instrument_component_spec` | That every gauge carries the `data-` attributes Stimulus writes into |
| `models/blueprint_spec` | Derived from `Operations.**catalogued**`, not `known`: a spec rig registers globally, so deriving from `known` makes it an unlockable machine nobody has priced and takes the whole catalogue down — **only in a full-suite run**, because nothing else loads `spec/support/loop_rig.rb`. That the unlock catalogue is **derived from the simulation's registries and never written down** — one part blueprint per registered part, one per operation type, one per content archetype — because a hand-maintained list drifts *silently*, leaving a new part simply unreachable. Also that a chassis id is scoped to its operation (`steam_engine/high_pressure`), so two machines that each name a frame `standard` cannot share an unlock, and that a real part id is not findable under the wrong kind. Plus **the gates**: every blueprint priced, bills denominated only in resources `content/` has, an unpriced blueprint raising rather than being free, and `materials: {}` as the written-down way to *be* free. **The achievement gate stubs nothing** — `Achievement.earned?` reads `awards`, so the spec opens the gate by granting a real `Award` and closes it by granting none |
| `requests/crews_spec` | The pre-match crew screen, and **validate, store, reset** — the same order `loadouts_spec` guards, for people. That every role is named including empty ones, that an unselected `<select>`'s `""` becomes an empty slot rather than `:""` (a posting for somebody with no name), that posting an injured minion is **refused rather than silently swapped**, and the four shapes `permit!` would admit. Note the assertion that reads `"Gauge Spanner"` and not `"Stoker's Shovel"`: **ERB escapes the apostrophe**, so asserting a label as written in `kit.rb` looks for something that cannot appear |
| `services/injury_list_spec` | The only consequence in this release that outlives its match. That `:mortal` alone reaches the list and the tier is **read from the event rather than re-derived** (the engine owns the ladder; a second copy here would fail in the direction of losing people forever); that a redelivered injury does not lengthen a sentence while a *later run* starts a fresh one; that the standin can never be listed, since an inexhaustible supply with a row against it would take the floor out from under every later match; and that recovery is derived from the run, so a replayed match takes the same person out for the same time |
| `support/reference_crew` | **The crew a spec runs a machine with, and deliberately nobody real.** Stoking is effort now, so a lever position is an instruction and what comes of it depends on who is carrying it out — an engine with no roster is worked by day-labourers and never raises steam (56 kPa, 0 rpm, against 608 kPa and 174.6 rpm for a competent hand). A spec that runs an engine and does not say who is working it measures the labour exchange rather than the machine. Pinning that to Jim would break every such spec on every balance tune, looking like a physics regression, so the fixture is flat 1.0 at every stat with no kit — capability exactly 1.0, the baseline every station's throughput is declared against. **`endurance` is the deliberate exception at `TIRELESS` (1e6)**: it divides fatigue accrual and never enters `capability`, so a huge value moves no throughput baseline at all while keeping a machine spec about the machine — a real hand at the firehole is spent in about five minutes and the fire then dies, which turned a 7000-tick boiler-burst example into a cold boiler and nobody hurt. **And a spec now has to DEPLOY the shift** — crew start in the crew quarters, not at a lever, so a run that posts nobody produces 0 kW and a 322 K firebox. `ReferenceCrew.deploy!(op)` is that move (one hand on the shovel, one on the oil round) and reproduces every balance figure recorded before crew capacity: 547.9 kPa and 495.5 kW against 547.8 and the same rpm. Tag a group `crew: :reference` to stub `Content.default`; **passing `content:` covers the build but not a restore**, since `Operation.from_h` never sees one, and `before(:all)` fires before the hook |
| `support/constructed` | **Build the state a claim needs instead of running a machine until it reaches one**, and the shared primitives both rigs sit on — `seed`, `body`, `wall`, `parcels_at`. ## The second spec to need a rig gets a shared one, in `support/`

**The rule: when a second spec file needs the same fixture, the fixture moves to
`spec/support/` in that commit rather than being copied.** Not the third, not "when it gets
painful" — the second. There are three rigs now and each pays for itself:

| Rig | For | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| `ReferenceCrew` | anything with a crew | the flat-1.0 hand, `PLAIN_STATS`, `deploy!`, the engine loadout |
| `PitRig` | anything with a mine | the collier, `build_pit`, `run!`, `deploy!`, `held`/`air`/`gas_pct` |
| `LoopRig` | the solver | a closed-loop operation with no topological order |
| `Conservation` | any balance assertion | `TOLERANCE`, which is a **contract** figure |

Copying is tempting because each copy is a two-line diff and the rig is a new file. What it
actually cost here: **nine mine specs carrying 531 lines of identical fixture**, replaced by a
126-line rig — and in those nine copies the collier's `endurance` had drifted into two values
with nothing saying which was deliberate. (It *was* deliberate, and is now
`CONTENT` against `TIRING_CONTENT` with the reason written down: bad air drains `fatigue`, so a
tireless hand makes every afterdamp claim pass while proving the opposite.) A difference that
matters is invisible when it is one number in the eighth copy of a paragraph.

### Take the rig's machine and keep your own people

Twelve files include `PitRig`; only eight use its collier. The other four — `peril`, `slip`,
`lever_travel`, `mine_tech` — stub `Content.default` with their own fixtures, because in those
the *crew is the experiment*: a comparison between a clumsy hand and a practised one, or between
five tiers of kit, is only a comparison of the hand or the kit if everything around it is held
still. **So they take the rig's pit, `run!` and `deploy!`, and keep their own content**, and each
says in one line why. That is the normal case rather than an exception — the rig exists to hold
the machine still so a spec can vary one thing.

A spec that needs neither is left alone: `passage_spec` has its own synthetic operation, and its
two bare mine builds deliberately take the *default* crew and *drawn* ground, which pinning
would change.

### What goes in a rig and what does not

A rig owns what is **incidental** — feeding the supply, walking the shift to its posts, reading
a parcel out of a node. It must not own a spec's **identity**: the match id, the seed and the
loadout stay at the call site, because those choose which machine is being measured and a
shared default silently re-points an example at a different one. `PitRig#build_pit` therefore
takes `id:` and `seed:` with no defaults, and omits `loadout:` entirely unless asked.

Two smaller rules that follow:

- **A rig helper returns the useful thing, not the convenient thing.** `run!` hands back the
  events, because the caller already holds the operation it passed in. A spec that wants to
  chain writes its own two-line wrapper (`mine_spec#working`).
- **A constant inside `describe` lands on `Object`.** Ruby has no example-group scope, so
  `FOO = …` in an `RSpec.describe` block is `Object::FOO` and two files declaring it overwrite
  each other — **and which wins depends on the randomised file order**. Renaming to something
  file-specific only works until the next person picks the same obvious name; it happened twice
  here with `STATS` → `PLAIN_HAND`. Put it in the rig. Ruby reports every live collision, so
  the derivation is to read the warnings:

```sh
bundle exec rspec 2>&1 | grep "already initialized constant"
```

### Why `minion_spec` builds its own rig

Every steam engine lever has `stiffness: Float::INFINITY`, so `ControlPoint#actuate` snaps
`actual` to `target` and **throws the minion's rate multiplier away before reading it**. The
engine's own specs therefore pass whether or not a minion is ever consulted. A bare rig with a
deliberately stiff lever is the only thing in the suite that can prove the seam is connected —
if you change how the crew reaches `actuate`, that is the file that notices.

## `support/loop_rig.rb`

A deliberately small operation that exercises the whole engine at once: a **closed loop**
(boiler → steam line → condenser → return line → boiler, which no topological order exists
for), pressure-driven phase change, conduction, ambient loss, and back-pressure. Its
diagnostics list is one of each instrument kind.

It is a **test fixture, not a game operation**. Prefer extending it over building a new rig.

## Tune a spec's constants through the SPEC's rig, not a scratch script

A scratch script and a spec diverge silently through **defaults nobody wrote down**, and the
divergence does not announce itself — it just produces a number that does not transfer.

Measuring the ashpan example's damper setting in a scratch script gave 35, and in the spec that
was still 4.4 kPa from the safety valve and could measure nothing. The cause was one line that was
never *written* in either place: the script set `cutoff: 40`, while `light_and_run` leaves cut-off
at its `ControlPoint` default of **100 — full gear**, which is a materially different engine
(517 kW against 364 kW at the same margin). Re-measured through `light_and_run` itself, the
answer was damper 30.

So when a value will end up inside an assertion, drive the sweep through the spec's own helper
and its own `engine` constructor. `Match.create` vs `SteamEngine.build`, the seed, and every
lever the helper does *not* set are all places the two rigs can quietly part company.

## Writing new specs

- **Conservation is the spec that catches real physics bugs.** Write it early, not last.
- Inject an in-memory `Content.build(...)` registry to isolate behaviour — a substance with no
  `phase:` cannot boil, so nothing turns into anything else while you test something unrelated.
- **Spec unused palette pieces too.** An upgrade slot nobody has exercised is one that will not
  work when it is first reached for.
- The suite is slow, dominated by the steam engine's long startup runs. Run one file while
  iterating.

## Debugging a conservation failure

1. Step one tick at a time, recording `Ledger.energy_balance(op.total_joules, op.ledger)`
   before and after.
2. Any tick where the balance moves by more than float noise is the culprit.
3. Diff **per-node** energy (`state[:joules] + Parcel.total_joules(state[:parcels])`) across
   that tick, alongside the ledger deltas.

Both conservation bugs found so far were invisible by inspection. Watch for a node with a huge
`heat_capacity` absorbing energy nothing resets, and for reactions, which conserve *enthalpy*
rather than temperature.
